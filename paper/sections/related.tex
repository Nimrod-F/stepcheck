\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Runtime orchestration and durable execution.} Triggerflow rethinks how serverless
workflows run~\cite{arjona2021triggerflow}, while durable-execution runtimes---Azure Durable
Functions~\cite{burckhardt2021durable}, Temporal~\cite{temporal}, and Beldi's transactional
guarantees for stateful serverless workflows~\cite{zhang2020beldi}---enforce recovery and
transactions \emph{during} execution. \tool{} is complementary: it statically checks the deployed
Step Functions artifact \emph{before} deployment with no new runtime, pursuing the same
failure-atomicity goal from the opposite end.

\textbf{Formal verification at other layers.} AWS uses TLA$^{+}$ to model-check service
designs~\cite{newcombe2015awsformal}, and Zelkova brings SMT reasoning to IAM access
policies~\cite{backes2018zelkova}. These verify an abstract model, the platform, or a different
artifact; \tool{} checks the executable \asl{} definition with linear passes suitable for CI.
Woflan~\cite{woflan}, BPMN Analyzer~\cite{bpmnanalyzer}, and
BProVe~\cite{bprove} verify control-flow soundness over Petri-net or BPMN models
and do so effectively on that class (Table~\ref{tab:woflan}); but they analyse a control-flow
abstraction, not the deployed \asl{} artifact, so data flow, retry, idempotency, and temporal
bounds fall outside their property language, and their single-sink criterion predates \asl{}'s
multi-terminal termination, cf.\ relaxed soundness~\cite{dehnert2001relaxed}.
Closest to our retry-safety check, Flux verifies idempotence of stateful serverless
functions~\cite{ding2023flux} and could \emph{supply} the per-task idempotency premises \tool{}'s
\code{SC3001} consumes.

\textbf{Behavioural types and Sagas.} Typestate and session types provide the basis for protocol
conformance~\cite{stromYemini1986typestate,honda2008multiparty}, and the Saga pattern is the
standard compensation model~\cite{garciaMolina1987sagas}; \tool{} adapts both to serverless task
graphs as edge-local declared checks and a declared-model reachability check.

\textbf{Data-flow and cloud validators.} Existing Step Functions validators check schema and path
syntax, but not field provenance
\cite{awsStatelint,aslValidator,awsValidateDefinition,awsStepFunctions}. 
AWS's \textsc{TestState} API~\cite{awsTestState} complements them
dynamically, executing states against author-supplied inputs and mocks;
its coverage is thus bounded by the tests written. \tool{} is the static
dual: with no inputs or mocks, its sound verdict quantifies over
\emph{every} execution---including paths no test exercises, such as the
unreachable branch behind the rediscovered \code{SC1110} bug.
Data-flow anomalies have
been studied for BPMN and \textsc{Bpel}~\cite{sun2006dataflow,trcka2009antipatterns}, but there
data is abstracted to named elements in a hand-built model; \tool{} tracks nested JSON document
shapes through \asl{}'s I/O pipeline as an abstract interpretation~\cite{cousot1977}. Serverless
formal foundations motivate the setting but do not provide static workflow
verification~\cite{jangda2019serverless}.
