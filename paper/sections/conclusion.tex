\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

Serverless workflow orchestration has become a backbone of cloud-native systems, yet
its workflows are untyped configuration whose correctness is checked only when they
run---and the tooling developers have stops at JSONPath \emph{syntax}. We presented
\tool, a static verifier whose centrepiece is the first \emph{sound} data-flow analysis
of the Amazon States Language: a flow-sensitive abstract interpretation over a
may-present lattice of document shapes that catches missing-field references with no
false positives and no annotations. Around it \tool{} adds seven further checks---%
structural, typed contracts, typestate, retry safety, concurrency interference,
retry/timeout budget, and Saga compensation---two of them for classes no \asl{} tool had
addressed, all compiling back to executable \asl{}. The key to analysing the workflows
that already exist is a two-layer model: sound native checks that need nothing beyond
\asl, paired with a lightweight annotation layer whose defaults are inferred from the
naming and resource conventions that pervade real workflows.

Our evaluation on 193 real Step Functions workflows (plus 66 CNCF) supports the three
research questions. \tool{} detects the targeted defect classes (100\% recall over 616
injected defects) where a standard schema validator detects none of the retry,
compensation, concurrency, or temporal defects, and surfaces 33 unbounded-callback
defects in unmodified workflows; the data-flow and concurrency analyses fire zero false
positives in the wild (RQ1). It recovers persistence at 86\% and idempotency at 62\% accuracy at zero authoring
cost against an agreement-checked gold set---enough to make compensation warnings mostly
actionable (81\% precision)---all surfaced as correctable warnings, and its typed tier
recovers two-thirds of injected missing-field defects, the remainder being ASL's
intrinsic opacity (RQ2); and it verifies a workflow in $\approx$39\,$\mu$s while adding
zero runtime overhead, with verified workflows running unmodified on real AWS Step
Functions (RQ3).

The broader message mirrors the history of type systems: just as static typing moved
classes of error from run time to compile time, lightweight static verification---rooted
in a sound data-flow core---can do the same for serverless orchestration, on the
platforms developers already use. Future work includes further frontends (SAM, CDK),
richer effect/ownership information to sharpen the compensation and concurrency analyses,
a precise element-level shape for \texttt{Map}/\texttt{Parallel} item contexts, and a
labelled-precision study of the concurrency and temporal warnings. We release \tool, the
corpus, and the evaluation harness to support replication and reuse.
