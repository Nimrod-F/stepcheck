\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We evaluate \tool{} on real serverless workflows: RQ1, does it detect the defect classes of
Figure~\ref{fig:intro}? RQ2, what does verification cost? RQ3, does the shared-IR design extend
beyond AWS? Controlled detection power (mutation) is kept separate from real-world evidence
(unmodified workflows and independently fixed bugs).

\subsection{Setup and corpus}
\label{sec:eval:corpus}

We mined \textbf{193} Step Functions workflows from two public AWS sample collections, taking every
parseable \asl{} definition and excluding build-configuration JSON. The corpus contains 1{,}355
state entries: 1{,}354 valid states (median 6, max 33) plus one malformed non-state
\texttt{QueryLanguage} entry reported as \code{SC0010}. It has 166 \texttt{Choice}, 64
\texttt{Map}, and 33 \texttt{Parallel} states; 84 workflows with retries; and 51 with catch
handlers. It also includes \textbf{5} JSONata-mode, \textbf{19} Distributed Map, and \textbf{18}
nested-\texttt{states:startExecution} workflows across nine AWS services. All experiments use a
Rust~1.96 release build on a commodity laptop.

\subsection{Detection in the wild}
\label{sec:eval:wild}

Run unmodified over the corpus, with optional inference enabled as a non-blocking adoption mode,
\tool{} flags \textbf{99 of 193} workflows. The native findings are small but concrete. Nine
workflows have a \texttt{Choice} with no \texttt{Default} (\code{SC0007}), a runtime-risk warning
because an input matching no rule fails. One workflow nests a machine-level \texttt{QueryLanguage}
directive inside \texttt{States} (\code{SC0010}). Another 33 callback tasks lack a timeout or
heartbeat (\code{SC6001}); each can wait up to the platform limit while holding business state open.
The sound \code{SC1101}, dead-guard \code{SC1110}, and
concurrency checks fire on none of the 193 (precision on curated examples); their power is exercised
below.

The semantic warnings surface adoption targets: \code{SC3001}: 36, \code{SC4001}: 117, and
\code{SC4010}: 28---not declared-tier claims until the facts are declared. Against human gold labels
(inter-annotator $\kappa\,{=}\,0.89$ idempotency, $0.99$ persistence) these inference-driven
warnings are precise where the inferred premise is reliable: among gold-covered findings,
\code{SC4001} is \textbf{33 of 37 (89\%)} true positive, and the effect-aware
\code{SC4010} is 6 of 6; \code{SC3001} is \textbf{7 of 7 (100\%)}. Overall,
\code{SC3001}/\code{SC4001} contribute \textbf{40 of 44 (91\%)} true positives. All three inferred
semantic warning codes contribute \textbf{46 of 50 (92\%)}. The downstream declared check \code{SC4011} is
silent on the wild corpus because no workflow declares a compensator. On the two built-in declared
Sagas (7 persistent steps), it reports zero findings; after a one-edge chain break that redirects
\texttt{RefundPayment} to failure, it reports exactly \texttt{CreateOrder} and
\texttt{ReserveInventory}, the upstream effects that would leak.

\subsection{Detection power versus existing validators}
\label{sec:eval:mut}
\label{sec:eval:baseline}

For each applicable workflow, \texttt{stepcheck mutate} injects one defect and re-verification
counts a detection only when the expected diagnostic increases over the unmutated baseline. Across
\textbf{616} mutants in six corpus-scale classes, \tool{} detects every injected defect
(Figure~\ref{fig:detection}a and Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). This is per-check reliability on
targeted operators, not a claim of complete bug coverage.

We compare against the validators developers already use---\texttt{statelint},
\texttt{asl-validator}, and AWS's authoritative \texttt{ValidateStateMachineDefinition}. This is a
complementarity test, not a superiority claim: those validators catch schema-expressible dangling
transitions and broken JSONPath syntax, but do not attempt unsafe retries, missing compensations,
races, or heartbeat/timeout violations. In the wild the two flag largely disjoint workflows
(Figure~\ref{fig:detection}b): \tool{} alone flags 36, the panel alone 55, mostly schema nits.

\subsection{Data-flow provenance: soundness, power, and reach}
\label{sec:eval:dataflow}

The central \code{SC1101} claim is no false positives. It fires zero times on the 193 unmodified
workflows. To exercise its power, the typed-tier mutator is seeded with declared input fields and
injects a missing-field read into each of the \textbf{126} workflows with a suitable site. \tool{}
detects \textbf{84 of 126 (67\%)}. An independent concrete execution oracle, implemented separately
from the analysis, confirms every one of the 84 absent on all acyclic reaching paths, with zero
counterexamples.

The check is not vacuous: it catches the \texttt{amount}/\texttt{total} miss validators accept, and
flow-sensitive misses through \texttt{ResultPath} merges that adjacent-task field-subset checks miss.
The undetected third are downstream of opaque task results, where the shape soundly collapsed to
$\top$.

The modeled fragment covers most real reads: of the corpus's genuine document-field references,
\textbf{92\%} use dotted access and resolve precisely, and \textbf{8\%} use bracket/wildcard/filter
syntax abstracted to $\top$ (\texttt{\$\$} context and \texttt{States.*} operands are not document
references; \texttt{stepcheck path-coverage}, full breakdown in the technical report).

\subsection{Real defects in the wild}
\label{sec:eval:realbugs}

We mined fix-commit pairs whose diff edits an \asl{} definition and replayed \tool{} across each fix.
On \textbf{7 of 39} pairs a pre-fix finding disappears after the fix; adjudication confirms
\textbf{6} genuine across five repositories, one an official AWS sample (Table~\ref{tab:realbugs});
four are semantic/logic classes no schema validator expresses. This is existence evidence, not a
prevalence estimate.

\subsection{Cost, overhead, and deployment}
\label{sec:eval:cost}
\label{sec:eval:roundtrip}

Verification is cheap: the eight passes average $\approx$\textbf{39}\,$\mu$s per workflow (median
$\approx$25\,$\mu$s, max $\approx$1.1\,ms), the whole corpus verifies in $\approx$\textbf{7.5}\,ms,
and the check adds no runtime component. Synthetic acyclic chains scale near-linearly: 100 states in
$\approx$0.2\,ms, 1{,}000 in $\approx$2.6\,ms, 10{,}000 in $\approx$31\,ms, 30{,}000 in
$\approx$0.13\,s. A topology stress sweep exercises harder shapes: a 512-branch \texttt{Parallel}
fan-out (2{,}051 states) verifies in $\approx$49\,ms, a 160-level nested \texttt{Map} (161 states) in
$\approx$11\,ms. The DSL stays concise (20 declarations expand to a 119-line annotated \asl{}
definition), and a DSL-verified order workflow deployed unmodified as an AWS Express machine reached
\texttt{SUCCEEDED}.

\subsection{Cross-format generalization: CNCF Serverless Workflow}
\label{sec:eval:cncf}

The CNCF front end parses \textbf{66} Serverless Workflow examples and lowers them to the same IR
without changing any pass. Structural, typestate, retry, compensation, concurrency, and temporal
checks apply unchanged; the ASL-specific JSONPath data-flow checks stay silent because CNCF uses
runtime expressions. Mutation detects every injected defect with a site (retry 4/4, structural 9/9,
concurrency 1/1, temporal 50/50), and where CNCF declares JSON Schemas \code{SC1010} uses them
exactly: a reordered schema-annotated order workflow is rejected with three contract errors.

\subsection{External validity: independent repositories}
\label{sec:eval:wildext}

To test whether the analyses overfit AWS's own samples, we ran \tool{} unmodified on \textbf{95}
further ASL definitions from \textbf{16} public repositories outside the AWS sample collections
(50 domain workflows across 13 repositories, the rest validator fixtures; script and manifest in the
artifact). The sound \code{SC1101} fires on a credit-card transaction-reversal workflow: its
\texttt{Pass} state reconstructs the document through \texttt{Parameters} into a closed record that
drops the \texttt{ledgerEntries} field a downstream \texttt{Map} iterates. This is a flow-sensitive
miss no adjacent-field check sees. The concurrency check flags an IoT firmware-upgrade workflow
whose three \texttt{Parallel} branches write one DynamoDB table. Declaring the two durable effects
of an independent payment-settlement pipeline through a sidecar promotes \tool{}'s finding to a
declared-tier \emph{error}: a settlement failure leaves an authorized hold that is never voided.
These hits, on workflows we neither authored nor curated, show the checks generalize beyond the
sample corpus.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
\label{sec:threats}
\tool{} verifies workflow definitions, not opaque Lambda or managed-service internals. Opaque
results and JSONata expressions outside the modeled direct-reference/object fragment lift to $\top$,
preserving soundness but losing precision. The
mutation operators are our own, so the 100\% figure is per-operator reliability against a targeted
fault population, not detection power against an independent one. The in-the-wild and real-fix
results (Sections~\ref{sec:eval:wild},~\ref{sec:eval:realbugs},~\ref{sec:eval:wildext}) supply the
complementary uncontrolled evidence. Declared-tier semantic results are relative to supplied
annotations, whose truth is outside \tool{}'s scope. The execution oracle cross-checks \code{SC1101}
along acyclic reaching paths only, so the loop-join fixpoint step rests on the convergence argument
(Section~\ref{sec:approach:dataflow}). The main corpus is public sample repositories and skews
small; proprietary industrial definitions are excluded, so the generated sweeps exercise scale and
topology but not industrial semantic diversity (extended limitations in the technical report). The
concurrency pass checks effects inside a child machine only when that child is itself checked. The
deployment smoke test uses one Express-mode emission, not Standard mode or a live callback.
